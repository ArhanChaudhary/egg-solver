More options and how it works

egg-solver proves that a solution is the shortest possible with a large table of lower bounds on how many moves positions need, which it prepares here in your browser the first time and then saves, so later visits start almost at once. Light (243 MB, the first choice on phones, tablets and devices with little memory) is quick to prepare but slower on hard cubes. Standard (486 MB) takes about ten seconds to prepare on a laptop. Faster (also 486 MB) takes a minute or two to prepare, then solves hard cubes faster. If one cannot be prepared (for example, without enough memory), the page goes back to a smaller one. Each kind is saved separately, so you can switch between them. Counting quarter turns needs solving data of its own (about a minute and a half to prepare on a laptop, and Faster takes 973 MB there), which is saved separately too.

Random cubes mostly need 17 or 18 moves; with all of a laptop's processor cores their shortest solution takes from under a second to some seconds, and short scrambles are solved at once. Every solution is checked on a separate model of the cube in this page.

Under Solutions you can ask for every shortest solution, or for longer ones too: the shortest is not always the easiest to turn. Two solutions that differ only in the order of turns of opposite faces (U D and D U) count as one, and a solution with a detour that undoes itself is left out. A list shows up to 2000 solutions, and stops after the time you set or when you press Stop, keeping what it has found (a stopped list says up to which length it is complete). A cube with symmetries is listed faster: the page searches one of each set of equivalent branches and turns the solutions it finds into the others.

For developers: instead of preparing the data here, you can load a table file made by egg-solver's command-line program, from h0 (30 MB) up to h6 (2 GB); files are checked against their checksum. See how to make a table.
