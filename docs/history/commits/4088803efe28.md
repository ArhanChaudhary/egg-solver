## Original experiment record

share: builds in stages use all threads, and no fresh pages

When threads wait for a cube's next iteration, its job list is extended
and sorted in stages by all of them (`build_task`). Traces of one cube
at a time (48 threads) showed iteration 17's build taking 1.2-1.5 ms and
iteration 18's 0.6 ms: the stages after the first ran on one thread, and
most of the rest was page faults. Fixes:

- A cube whose build tasks were all claimed (between stages) was dropped
  from the open list, and the next stage did not list it again: only the
  thread that closed a stage found the next one. Cubes now carry a
  `listed` flag (changed under the list's write lock) and every stage
  opening lists the cube.
- Dead cubes were removed from the open list with `try_write`, which
  rarely succeeded under the read locks of the threads looking for work,
  so every refill kept taking the lock. The removal now waits for it.
- `std`'s RwLock queues readers behind a waiting writer in the kernel:
  each publication made the 47 polling threads sleep for tens of us. It
  is replaced by a reader-writer spin lock (spinning, then yielding).
- Build buffers were reallocated as they moved between the builder's
  scratch lane and the cube, and new cubes started with empty ones:
  ~420 page faults per cube, most of them in the stages (about 1 us per
  4 KiB page). Job buffers are now allocated at full size and only
  swapped; the other buffers of a staged build come from a pool-wide
  free list (`Bufs`), and serial builds use the builder's own (those of
  the pool were last written by other cores). The serial sort uses them
  instead of allocating its keys.
- When the jobs are those of the last iteration (iterations 18+), the
  staged sort takes the statistics straight from the atomics (the serial
  copy to the jobs took ~90 us); the serial copy is prefetched.
- Jobs whose bounds exceed the iteration (they would be skipped) are
  sorted after the others and not claimed (claims cover the feasible
  prefix; at least one job). The order of the feasible jobs is that of
  `sort_jobs`, as before.
- The trace (CUBE_TRACE) prints, per iteration, its run, when its claims
  ran out, nodes, jobs, donations and splits, and per build stage its
  tasks, threads and times.
