## Original experiment record

b2: one resolve for both lookup kinds, advance unchanged (negative, -3.8%)

resolve_other and resolve_moved are merged into one resolve driven by a
per-lane flag: whether the pending lookup is the other side's (else the
moved side's, with the other side's value in k_po). The visit no longer
matches on the phase; advance keeps its move-kind branches
("resolve-only" variant of neg/b2-unified-resolve).

Why: the TAGE simulation of main put 0.285 mispredicts per node on the
phase dispatch, the largest single site.

48 threads (bench /mnt/huge/h11 stream 20 30 48 16, interleaved, 3 runs,
solves/s):

| build              | run 1 | run 2 | run 3 | median |
|--------------------|-------|-------|-------|--------|
| main fe58740       | 684.9 | 685.1 | 682.7 | 684.9  |
| unified resolve    | 661.0 | 659.0 | 651.4 | 659.0  |

Change: -3.8% (median). 1-thread solves/s: not recorded.

Conclusion: slower. On real Zen 5 hardware the phase dispatch is
predicted well (the simulator overstates it), so a unified resolve only
adds masked work to every resolution.

Source: scratchpad b2/neg-unified-resolve-only.patch, made against
fe58740; applies cleanly. Its numbers are also listed on
opt/neg-unified-resolve.
Numbers are the agent's own (not re-measured for this record). Not merged; recorded per the rule that every candidate gets a branch.
