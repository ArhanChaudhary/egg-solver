## Original experiment record

Merge opt/p5-sched: hot-loop latency, 4 patches (+3.2% 48T, +1.6% 1T)

Round 5 lane-scheduling stack (src/lanes.rs only):

- regs: advance keeps the depth and the top node's todo/lb/r in
  registers (written through to the stack), so the exhaustion/pop
  branch no longer waits on a store-to-load forward.
- fresh-child: after a push, the child's first child (always an X move)
  is generated from the node held in registers; rare cases go to an
  out-of-line advance_cold.
- no-idle: a new cube is advanced to its first lookup out of line; the
  dispatch has two arms and there is no third inlined advance.
- node-count: stats.nodes is counted in a local and added when advance
  returns.

The search is unchanged:
- random:1000:2026 gives nodes/cube 3295521 and lookups/cube 2785769,
  with lengths 15:1 16:35 17:270 18:662 19:32;
- depth18 gives 3607979 / 3053364, lengths 18:100.
check.sh passes (exit 0).

Instructions per node (cachegrind, x86-64-v3 build): 170.19 -> 164.51
(-3.3%). Mispredicts are unchanged (TAGE sim, 0.97 per node). The gain
comes from shorter latency on mispredicted branches and fewer µops.

Negatives (agent A/Bs, all search-identical; patches in the scratchpad):
- Phase-bucketed scheduling (FIFO pointer arrays) at 20 lanes: -6.1%
  48T and about -5% 1T. At 24 lanes -3.4%; at 28 lanes -1.7%.
  Mispredicts drop from 0.97 to 0.66 per node, but instructions rise
  10.9%. Changing each lane's revisit gap costs more than the dispatch
  saves.
- Two lane groups: -8.4% 48T and -4.7% 1T.
- Bucketing with prefetcht0, or with prefetch of the next lane's
  state: worse.
- fresh-child alone on 35c608c: neutral.
