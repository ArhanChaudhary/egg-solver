EXPERIMENT (c7): final-iteration dataset (C7DUMP) and offline policy evaluator (dseval)

Diagnostic only. With C7DUMP=, every lane runs each iteration exhaustively, keeps the first solution, and at the end of the last iteration dumps per (job, root child): nodes N of the child's subtree at T, offset F of its first solution (0 if none), and its T-1 features (per-child histogram of lookup values, nodes). Job roots are forced to the normal side (counts then equal a forced-normal main exactly: 3417325 nodes/cube on the first 300 of random:1000:2026, checked). dseval replays orderings: cost = sum of N before the first item with a solution + its F.

Findings (300 cubes of 1000:2026 / 600 of 3000:777; % of total nodes vs the dataset's main order):

  • oracle (solution item first): -46.9%: the last iteration is ~47% of all nodes.
  • per-child glob (all (job, child) items by child statistic): -10.8% / -5.1%.
  • top 10% of items glob, then the rest by job: -10.1% / -6.6%; with 2-octave merge -10.3% / -7.5%.
  • c4-like (top 3 per job, rest by job): -6.0% / -2.4%.
  • per-face glob (items (job, face)): -4.8% (2026): per-child granularity matters.
  • whole jobs by max child, children sorted inside: -0.2%: reordering inside a job is worthless.
  • base of the term (8..256) flat; 2 worse; normalising by subtree size worse; job-sum mixing worse.
  • In the real build these gains shrank (free side choice of the solver, stale attribution of root-level lookups, quantisation); c7-childsplit reaches -6.3% / -7.2% / -6.9% on the full sets.
