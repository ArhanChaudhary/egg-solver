## Original experiment record

a2: side choice tie-break by the other side's bound (negative, -0.07% nodes)

make_node chooses the side (normal or inverse cube) with fewer allowed
moves. This patch compares count * 64 + bound instead, i.e. ties are
broken by the other side's lower bound (cube48opt-style). Variants with a
real weighting (count * K + bound, K = 1, 2, 4) and the reverse tie-break
were measured as well.

Why: on a tie, branching on the side whose other side is tighter could
prune earlier.

Nodes and lookups per cube on random:1000:2026 (bench <table>
random:1000:2026 1000 48 16; lengths identical in every variant):

| variant                       | nodes/cube | change | lookups/cube | change |
|-------------------------------|------------|--------|--------------|--------|
| main fe58740 (count only)     | 3295521    |        | 2785769      |        |
| count*64 + bound (this patch) | 3293072    | -0.07% | 2783649      | -0.08% |
| reverse tie-break             | 3365403    | +2.12% | 2822318      | +1.31% |
| count*1 + bound               | 3291910    | -0.11% | 2784127      | -0.06% |
| count*2 + bound               | 3295714    | +0.01% | 2786231      | +0.02% |
| count*4 + bound               | 3293690    | -0.06% | 2784353      | -0.05% |

48-thread and 1-thread solves/s: not measured (search-order change
judged on nodes/cube).

Conclusion: no effect. Every variant is within 0.1% (noise from a changed
traversal); only the reverse tie-break moves the count, by +2.1%. The
move count dominates the subtree size and the bound adds nothing.
