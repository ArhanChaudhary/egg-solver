share: few cubes in progress; data structures

With few cubes in progress (at most threads / 8: one cube at a time with all threads, or the end of a batch) the iterations are short and their ends set the latency. Traces of one cube at a time: iteration 15 (243 jobs) spent ~230 us of ~270 us in its tail, iteration 16 ~100-300 us.

  • Longest prefixes early: as soon as stream mode's prefixes are 2 moves (iteration 15 with h11), the jobs get 4 (43254 jobs): more and smaller jobs share an iteration better, and the extension is done once (by two moves in stages, one parent per task; the counts per parent come from a table).
  • Builds are staged whenever few cubes are in progress (threads are about to wait), not only once threads wait: iteration 15's three-level extension took 1.4 ms serially when the last thread of iteration 14 found nobody hungry yet.
  • Threads stay with (at most 2) unsolved cubes they have nothing to do on, and claim their next jobs there without searching the open list: no Arc clone, parts CAS, node-count RMWs and list lock per thread and iteration. Kept cubes are left once solved.

Data structures and synchronisation (from the pass over share.rs):

  • Mailbox pieces: Mutex<Option<Piece>> with a Vec<Node> allocated per donation is replaced by a slot with a fixed [Node; MAXD] path, accessed as the IDLE/WANT/FILL/FULL state machine orders it (the donor that moved WANT to FILL writes it, the owner reads it once FULL).
  • Claimed jobs are prefetched when claimed (their lines and their statistics', for writing): they were written by other threads (the sorted list by the build's tasks), and lanes start them one after another; before, only the next job was fetched.
  • Rarely written fields of a cube (listed, the node counts summed by leaving threads, trace counters) and the pool's buffer free lists are on lines of their own, away from read-mostly fields.
  • Checked, unchanged: the open list is read on refills that find no claimed or kept work (in a batch ~30 scans per cube), so a seqlock or epoch-published slots would not pay; spare (twice per cube), bufs (twice per staged build), result (once per cube) and counters (at the end) are uncontended mutexes. The per-job statistics are written by the thread that claimed the chunk (16 per line, chunks of up to 64). No SeqCst; on x86-64 the other orderings cost nothing (plain loads and stores, locked RMWs either way).
