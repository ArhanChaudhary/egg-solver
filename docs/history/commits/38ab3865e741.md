## Original experiment record

DIAGNOSTIC (not for merging): counting global allocator in bench and solve

perf book "Heap Allocations" and "Alternative Allocators": do allocations
matter here? A #[global_allocator] wrapper around System counts allocs,
reallocs, bytes and allocations of >= 128 KiB, printed per run (ALLOC
line on stderr). valgrind's DHAT cannot run the AVX-512 build.

Per cube on main 7608a28 (1T bench slots; solve with 2 threads):
| run | cubes | allocs | reallocs | bytes | >= 128 KiB |
|-----|-------|--------|----------|-------|------------|
| bench stream 5+30 s, 1T, 24 lanes | 850 | 9.9 | 3.3 | 888 KB | 2.2 |
| bench share random:500:2026, 1T | 500 | 5.7 | 3.0 | 432 KB | 1.0 |
| solve -t 2 -c 1, random_state 100 | 100 | 25.7 | 3.1 | 106 KB | 0.22 |
| solve -t 2 -c 0, random_state 300 | 300 | 25.2 | 3.0 | 245 KB | 0.57 |
| solve -t 2 -c 1, random_move_15f 300 | 300 | 21.9 | 2.1 | 40 KB | 0.06 |

strace -f -c over a whole 1T stream run (5+30 s): 25 mmap, 14 munmap, 6
madvise, 267 mprotect, 4 brk; share 1T 300 cubes: 117 memory syscalls in
all; solve -t 2 -c 1 100 cubes: 113, plus 101 writes (one per cube).
perf stat: 24.8K page faults per 1T stream run, nearly all at start-up.
The 1T cpu-clock profile has 0.03% of samples in libc (malloc, free,
memset, memmove together). Conclusion: no allocator or buffer-reuse change
can gain measurably (not timed).
