## Original experiment record

Lane-interleaved explicit-stack IDA* for throughput; streaming benchmark

New src/lanes.rs: each thread runs N independent lanes, each solving its
own cube with an explicit-stack DFS that generates one child at a time
(cube48opt-style). Lanes advance in lockstep with batched lookups: (1)
every idle lane advances to its next child and prefetches the lookup of the
non-moved side (or the moved side when a half turn inherits the other
bound), (2) first lookups are resolved and second lookups prefetched, (3)
second lookups are resolved and the child pushed or pruned. This keeps many
DRAM misses in flight per core, and because children are generated one at
a time, sibling-face pruning now skips the lookups of pruned siblings
(lookups/cube 5.24M -> 3.99M). Search semantics (masks, tight-bound tricks,
side choice, back-jumps, job ordering) mirror solver.rs.

Benchmark changes: the batch metric is dominated by the end-of-batch tail
(and with lanes, ~3000 cubes are in flight at once), so bench now has a
streaming mode: an endless deterministic stream of random states (seed
2026), counting solves completed in a 30s window after a 20s warmup.
bench.sh reports the median of 3 runs. Batch mode still verifies all
solutions; known-depth sets 16..19 (100 each) all solve at the right length.

Lane-count scan (stream, 3s warmup/15s window, so absolute values for high
lane counts are inflated by start-up transients): 0: 287, 8: 278, 12: 329,
16: 369, 24: 419, 32: 450, 48: 489, 64: 506, 96: 500, 128: 486, 192: 457.
With proper warmup (20s), 32 and 64 lanes are within noise; 32 is the
default.
