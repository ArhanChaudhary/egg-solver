## Original experiment record

symstart: one job per class of equivalent prefixes for symmetric inputs

For a cube with a symmetry (t X t^-1 = X), many job prefixes reach
equivalent positions (conjugates of each other, or of their inverses, all
equally far from solved). Like nissy's task filter (symmetry_mask over its
4-move task tree), nxopt's -s (first move only) and Rokicki's "drop
symmetry duplicates in the first levels", the job lists now keep one job of
each class (src/symstart.rs):

- `keep`: for each symmetry t of the cube, a job p is dropped if
  canon(t(p)) < p (moves conjugated by t, opposite-face pairs reordered U
  before D, lexicographic on move indices). No cube is touched.
- `dedup`, only for a cube that also has an antisymmetry
  (t X t^-1 = X^-1): exact classes (least of the 96 conjugates of the
  position and of its inverse) of each extension level of at most 8192
  jobs; the smallest job of each class is kept. This catches the
  superflip's antisymmetry (it is its own inverse and commutes with every
  move: X p ~ X p^-1) and accidental equivalences.

Correctness: a job is dropped only if a lexicographically smaller job of the
same length reaches an equivalent position. The smallest prefix of a
canonical optimal solution, and each of its own prefixes, can never be
dropped (a smaller equivalent prefix would extend to a smaller prefix of an
optimal solution). The search inside a job (both sides, premoves) is
unchanged. Dropping fewer jobs (no filter, or `keep` without `dedup`) is
always safe.

Applied in stream mode (Lane::extend_jobs; the filter is in the cold
RunState), share mode (CubeCtx) and solver.rs. With a filter, every child
looks up its own inverse bound (an X' child's X sibling may be gone). In
share mode this includes a cube's first pass (the iterations up to 16 on
3-move jobs, 085a9fd), whose jobs are then claimed in extension order
(5cce44d). A parallel extension (`build_task`) runs with a filter that only
`keep`s (its tasks' offsets count the kept children, `kept_children`; the
tasks check the count); a filter that dedups extends serially, since its
classes span the tasks.

Port of x-h48's fe055f2 (written on 55e048e) to main fe926ed: s8's names,
pedantic clippy, the first pass, the portable backends (symstart only uses
the common Cube API). Two changes from fe055f2, both from measurements on
the new base:
- Cubes with only an antisymmetry are not filtered. For them neither rule
  drops a job (an antisymmetry relates X p to positions with p on the
  inverse side, which are not jobs): identical nodes on all 6 of the
  timing set, and the unit test's cube keeps all 43254 4-move jobs. The
  dedup then only cost time: ~0.75 ms per cube at 1T (0.7-1.0x), and
  1.5-3.2x their time at 48T -c 1 with the serial build.
- Cubes with only a symmetry get `keep` alone: on the 7 of the timing set,
  `dedup` dropped nothing beyond `keep` (1656 of 3240 3-move jobs either
  way) and took ~0.4 ms per cube at 1T. Without dedup their extension runs
  in parallel in share mode. With x-h48's version as ported (5cce44d, one
  screening round at 48T -c 1) they were slower than main (0.46-1.03x per
  cube, one 3.1x outlier) in spite of -33% nodes; now 1.30x (below).
- (Unchanged: the superflip has both, and dedup takes its 3-move jobs from
  60 to 44 and its 4-move ones from 553 to 380.)

Random states: SymFilter::new is 95 conjugations per cube (~0.3 us) and
