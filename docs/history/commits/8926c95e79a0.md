## Original experiment record

share: job and build buffers touched when allocated (no page faults while a pool warms up)

Share mode's builds were meant to touch no fresh pages (l6-share-a), but
they still did while a pool warmed up. Job buffers change hands: the serial
extension of a cube's first build swaps the builder's own buffer into the
cube, and builds swap the cube's buffer with pooled ones. A vector that was
only reserved keeps the pages of its unused tail fresh. With CUBE_TRACE=1,
solve -c 1 at 48T, the mean parallel extension to 4-move jobs (T = 17, 43254
children) took 0.20-0.26 ms instead of 0.04 in the first 50 cubes (11 ms
instead of 0.7 ms of task time), and ~0.1 ms up to cube ~150. On
random_move_17f (200 cubes), the mean T = 17 build by groups of 50 cubes was
0.308, 0.188, 0.209 and 0.120 ms.

- `touched`: a vector reserved at full size, written once, then cleared.
  It is used for the job and sort buffers of staged builds (`Bufs::sized`,
  `Pool::bufs`), and for an iteration's job and statistics buffers
  (`Inner::touched`, also when `fresh` finds no spare one).
- `WARM_BUFFERS` = 3: the first three threads of a pool each put a touched
  `Inner` into `spare` and a touched `Bufs` into `bufs`, so the first cubes
  find them.
- A worker's own buffers (`Bufs::own`): the job buffer (it changes hands)
  and the 43 KB key buckets are touched; the serial sort keys grow on
  demand. Touching only the job buffer left the early extensions as slow
  (0.18-0.26 ms). strace shows why: glibc had raised its mmap threshold
  after a 4.9 MB block was freed at startup, so the big buffers come from
  the threads' malloc arenas. Those then grew page by page (mprotect)
  during the first builds. With GLIBC_TUNABLES glibc.malloc.top_pad=4 MB
  and no extra touch, the extension took 0.04 ms. Writing the 43 KB grows
  each arena when its thread starts. Touching all sort buffers as well
  works too, but costs 0.73 ms of pool start instead of 0.42.

After the change, the mean T = 17 build on random_move_17f is 0.145, 0.116,
0.118 and 0.120 ms per 50 cubes (extension 0.046 ms, then 0.037).

Costs: a 48-thread pool takes 0.42 ms longer to start (solve -t 48 -c 1 -n 1
on a 1-move scramble, 8 runs each: 2.42 vs 1.99 ms). It keeps ~0.6 MB per
thread plus 3 x 2 MB more resident. Sets solved in the first pass (T <= 16:
15/16-move scrambles, depth-15/16 states, 200 cubes) pay that once:
+0.5..+3.4% per cube.

Lengths are identical cube for cube to fe926ed on the check sets (short
1000, random_move_15f 200, random_state_depth15 200, random_state 500,
depth18 3000; -c 1 and -c 0). check.sh passes (fmt, clippy -D warnings,
tests, other backends). One exception: the aarch64 tests under qemu fail
about 1 run in 4-6, on fe926ed as well (a debug_assert in `release` in
share_protocol_with_tiny_search, or a QEMU internal SIGSEGV).
