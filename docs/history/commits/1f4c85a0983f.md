## Original experiment record

share: in iteration tails, a thread splits its own lanes only while it has at most 8 active

In an iteration's tail there are no jobs left to claim. A lane whose job
ended then split a busy lane of its own thread, so every thread kept all
24 lanes active. Each round visits them all, so every lane advanced at
the pace of a fully loaded thread, about 0.36 us per node, while the
remaining jobs' long chains set the tail's length (~0.1-0.15 ms per
iteration at 48 threads). Each split also loses the bounds that the
split node learns from its children in the other part.

Now a lane splits another lane of its thread only while the thread has
at most `TAIL_LANES` (8) active lanes. Otherwise it goes idle, and the
remaining lanes run faster. Idle threads still take donated pieces (at
remaining depth 5 or more).

c9's screening on its first-pass stack (48 threads, 500 random states,
3 holds; -c 1 ms/cube (nodes), -c 0 ms/cube):

| TAIL_LANES | -c 1          | -c 0  |
|------------|---------------|-------|
| off        | 1.776 (3.82M) | 1.052 |
| 4          | 1.700 (3.62M) | 1.058 |
| 8          | 1.696 (3.66M) | 1.051 |

Other tail knobs were within noise: chunk divisor 4/8, no local splits
while threads are hungry, up to 4 donations per round, remote splits at
remaining depth 4.
