## Original experiment record

NEGATIVE c10: dord (not merged)

NEGATIVE (c10): a one-move extension writes its children in sorted order (dord)

Idea: the T16 -> T17 children inherit their parent's statistic, so their
sort order is known before they exist. Sort the 3240 parents (two radix
sorts, one per job_weight class), merge the classes' runs, and let the
extension tasks write each child to its final position; no keys,
scatter or bucket stages (~75 of the T17 build's ~125 us at 48T). The
order is identical to sort_serial's (unit test
placed_children_match_sort_serial, random stats with zeros and ties).

Result (48T, 2 holds each, paired vs AB = first pass + TAIL_LANES on
735dd25; ms/cube):

| test                 | AB            | dord          | change |
|----------------------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| -c 1 random_state 500| 1.696 1.705   | 1.715 1.788   | +3.0%  |
| -c 0 random_state 500| 1.044 1.055   | 1.051 1.067   | +0.9%  |
| -c 1 15f 200         | 0.195 0.194   | 0.197 0.190   | -0.5%  |
| -c 1 12-14-move 300  | 0.153 0.151   | 0.146 0.149   | -2.9%  |
| -c 1 depth15 200     | 0.177 0.173   | 0.174 0.175   | -0.3%  |

Probable cause: order_children is serial work at the barrier (two
3-pass radix sorts of up to 3240 keys and a branchy merge) and the
scattered writes of the children; it costs about what the parallel
stages did. A parallel merge (or one sort keyed by stat with the
rounding ties handled) might still pay; not tried.
