## Original experiment record

NEG lanes: sort_jobs and sort_runs by radsort

sort_jobs (LSD radix sort of (key, index) pairs, 3 passes of 11/11/10
bits that skip uniform digits, then a gather) and sort_runs (one counting
pass, counts fused into the loop that builds the entries) replaced by
radsort::sort_by_key (stable LSD radix sort, 8-bit digits, a fresh scratch
buffer per call). Same order (checked in the micro-benchmark). Rejected:
2.2-2.6x slower per sort, -1.5% 1T stream throughput.

Micro-benchmark (d1-x/sortbench, 1T slot, median of 300; voracious_radix_sort
1.2 voracious_stable_sort with Radixable impls for comparison; identical
output for all three):
| sort | n | ours | radsort | voracious |
|------|---|------|---------|-----------|
| sort_jobs | 43254 | 158 us | 418 us (2.6x) | 560 us (3.5x) |
| sort_jobs | 3240 | 15.0 us | 22.7 us (1.5x) | 33.0 us (2.2x) |
| sort_runs | 120000 | 78.7 us | 109 us (1.4x) | 158 us (2.0x) |
| sort_runs | 20000 | 12.3 us | 17.3 us (1.4x) | 25.6 us (2.1x) |
| sort_runs | 2000 | 1.4 us | 1.9 us (1.4x) | 3.3 us (2.4x) |
(sort_runs "ours" includes a separate counting pass here; in lanes.rs the
counts are fused into the producer loop: 51.9 vs 112 us at 120000 without
it.) radsort sorts the 12-byte jobs in place through 4 byte passes; ours
moves 8-byte keys in 3 passes and gathers the jobs once.
