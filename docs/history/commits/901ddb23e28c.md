## Original experiment record

c4: whole-job iterations in canonical job order (negative, +2.6% nodes)

In c4's whole-job iterations (the iterations right after the jobs are
extended), the jobs run in canonical order instead of by decreasing
inherited statistic (whole_key = 0).

Why: test whether the statistic order is worth it, and whether random
job access is what makes setup_job expensive.

Measured on c4 variant v3a (stream window, bench stream on ff2b7a4):

| order                           | nodes/cube | setup_job share of samples |
|---------------------------------|------------|----------------------------|
| by statistic (c4)               | 3117126    | 2.06%                      |
| canonical (this patch)          | 3198965    | 2.06%                      |

Change: +2.6% nodes. 48-thread and 1-thread solves/s: not measured.

Conclusion: the order matters; for 17-move cubes the last iteration is a
whole-job one. And canonical order does not make setup_job any cheaper,
so random job access is not its cost.
