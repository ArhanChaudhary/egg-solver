share: in iteration tails, a thread splits its own lanes only while it has at most 8 active

In an iteration's tail there are no jobs left to claim. A lane whose job ended then split a busy lane of its own thread, so every thread kept all 24 lanes active. Each round visits them all, so every lane advanced at the pace of a fully loaded thread, about 0.36 us per node, while the remaining jobs' long chains set the tail's length (~0.1-0.15 ms per iteration at 48 threads). Each split also loses the bounds that the split node learns from its children in the other part.

Now a lane splits another lane of its thread only while the thread has at most TAIL_LANES (8) active lanes. Otherwise it goes idle, and the remaining lanes run faster. Idle threads still take donated pieces (at remaining depth 5 or more).
