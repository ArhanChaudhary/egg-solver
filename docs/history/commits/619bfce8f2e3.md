## Original experiment record

diag: prune outcome of each lookup by the node's bound on that side

DIAGNOSTIC (not for merge). Against cb8cb70 (= fc6d9c9's tree).

Counts every resolved lookup by its kind (other side of X; moved side of
X, X2, X') and by r - bound of the node on the looked-up side (`lo` for
the other side; a new `Node::lm` for the moved side). It also counts
whether the child was pruned (value > r - 1). bench prints the table
after a batch.

random:200:2026 (7 threads, 20 lanes), counts (pass / prune):
| lookup    | r-bound 0          | 1                  | 2                | 3+               |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|------------------|------------------|
| other (X) | 43.7M / 294.0M     | 17.6M / 35.1M      | 4.76M / 5.91M    | 1.30M / 1.83M    |
| moved X   | 6.94M / 23.5M      | 17.1M / 12.0M      | 6.03M / 0.24M    | 1.45M / 0.09M    |
| moved X2  | 4.27M / 13.8M      | 15.2M / 10.1M      | 5.71M / 0.18M    | 1.46M / 0.06M    |
| moved X'  | 6.92M / 18.0M      | 16.9M / 11.8M      | 5.87M / 0.18M    | 1.39M / 0.07M    |

What follows:
- The other side's bound says little about whether the X child's
  lookup prunes. Even at r - lo = 2 it prunes 55%: the table is far from
  consistent below its base, where code 0 falls back to the group
  minimum and the corner depth. So issuing an X child's moved-side
  lookup together with the other side's when the other side looks
  loose wastes over half of them (cf. neg/m4-pair-lookups).
- The moved side is predictable by class (77% / 41% / 4% prune), but
  the class is high-entropy itself. Feeding it to the predictor (a
  per-class dispatch or an early branch) would add about as many
  early mispredicts as it removes late ones.
