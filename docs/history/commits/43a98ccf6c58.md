## Original experiment record

NEGATIVE c10: first-pass-order (not merged)

NEGATIVE / NOTES (c10): first-pass job order and start target (screening knobs)

Knobs on top of AB (first pass + TAIL_LANES, 735dd25), in the diff below
(CUBE_PASS_SORT, CUBE_PASS_MIN, CUBE_PASS_BIG; not for merging as is):
- sort1: the pass's jobs ordered by an exact bounds bucket (largest bound,
  then smallest; a counting sort over a byte computed by the extension
  tasks, claims mapped through an order index; no job_weight factor).
- sort2: no sort: the feasible jobs in extension order (same index).
- min13: no first pass from a target below 13 (T12 runs as before).
- big (CUBE_PASS_BIG): the last phase's largest trees claimed before the
  low phases. Not screened.

48T, 2 holds each, paired vs AB (ms/cube, solve -c 1 unless noted):

| test                  | sort1  | sort2  | min13  |
|-----------------------|--------|--------|--------|
| random_state 500      | +6.4%  | +0.8%  | +0.3%  |
| random_state -c 0     | +0.1%  | -0.3%  | +0.7%  |
| random_move_15f 200   | -8.5%  | -6.4%  | -1.8%  |
| 12-14-move scr. 300   | -9.9%  | -10.9% | -24.7% |
| random_state_depth15  | -8.3%  | -10.9% | -2.0%  |

sort1 is a negative: the exact bounds order is worse than c9's
(statistic from the bounds times job_weight) on random states. sort2 and
min13 help short cubes only (the pass's first build is cheaper without
the serial sort; cubes of 12 moves skip the 3-move build). The cxl patch
(lanes of solved cubes refilled together) gets most of the short-cube
gain without a cost on random states; sort2/min13 on top of it are
untested.
