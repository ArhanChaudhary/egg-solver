## Original experiment record

neg: page-pin the hot code and stack of both binaries (stream loop, its helpers, share)

This variant pins, at fixed page offsets:
- the stream loop: STREAM_LINE, build variable CUBEOPT_STREAM_LINE;
- its five out-of-line helpers, at 7608a28's `bench` offsets;
- the round loop;
- the share worker's stack: Worker at 0x040, `rounds` entered at 0xe38.
The stream thread's stack is not pinned: pinning it cost about 0.5%.

It gains share mode but loses stream against main, so it is not merged as
is. Alternating pairs, 1T stream 10 60 1 24 (ratio variant/main):
- vs 7608a28: +0.3 -1.0 +1.15%;
- vs 585b3b6: -0.7% (1 pair).
- With the stream stack pinned as well (sl640, vs 7608a28): -0.2 -0.5 -0.7%.
- Screening 10 page offsets of the stream loop (0x180..0x3c0, 2 pairs
  each, A-first): -0.1 .. -1.4%, best 0x280/0x2c0 at -0.5%.
- With the helpers at main's page offsets (v2a) instead: 1T -1.5 -1.1%,
  48T -1.4 -2.1%.
- Same binary, stream frame base moved: +0.6 -0.1 +0.8%. A/A control of
  ab1t pairs: +0.6 +0.1 +0.1 +0.5% bias toward the second-launched run.

Share mode gains:
- bench share at 48T: +1.8% / +3.5% (2 holds vs 585b3b6);
- bench share at 1T: +2.2% (1 pair), and +2.3 / +1.5% for v2a.
-c 1 at 48T: noisy, within the noise.

Conclusion: main's stream layout is a good one, and fixing page offsets
alone does not reproduce it. The relative distances between the stream
loop and its helpers also differ. s9-pin-v2 instead pins only `solve` and
leaves `bench` byte-identical.
