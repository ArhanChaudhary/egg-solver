vcube technique inventory

Historical snapshot. Its source, timings and pending statements belong to the recorded experiment, not the current checkout. Documentation home.

Source read in full: vcube src/* (avx2_cube.h, sse_cube.h, types.h, cube.h/.cpp, cube6.h, nxprune.h/.cpp, nxprune_generator.h, nxsolve.h/.cpp, vc-optimal.cpp, alloc.h/.cpp, util.h/.cpp, const-cubes.h, gen-const-cubes.cpp), README.md, doc/speffz.md, CMakeLists.txt, tests/. Ours = egg-solver (then named cube-opt) main e7b094e; the file:line references and names are from e7b094e, before the s8 series' renames. vcube paths are relative to vcube's src/.

Verdicts: done (we have it, or a stronger form), n/a (does not apply to an H48 h11 48-symmetric single-coset table, or not on a throughput path), gap (applies, we lack it).

Status: complete (main 387034c). No change was merged: the cocsep compaction is neutral (gain within code/data placement effects). The negative experiments and diagnostics with numbers are the neg/xvc-* branches in the negative-experiment index.

Summary. About 50 vcube techniques. Nearly all are done in our solver (often in a stronger form) or do not apply to a 48-symmetric single-coset H48 table (the cube6 axis lookups and their "+1 if all equal" / axis-tight rules have single-coset analogs we already use: NO_HALF, X2/X' twins). Per-op costs are equal (move, premove) or lower (child cubes 12.5 vs 53.8 Ir, index 45 vs 69 Ir). Gaps: C12 (15 duplicate depth-4 jobs, 0.035%) and C13 (prefix-cube table, <= 0.4% Ir) are too small to pay. B6 (vcube's compact corner sym table) does not carry over as such, but its point does: the cocsep table's L2 footprint costs ~1.6% per +33% (s4); 18-bit packed entries (p18) gained +1.6% at 1T layout-averaged on e7b094e but nothing in its dense form on later code; the row-padded form read 4 bits up (p18d) gained +1.2% at 1T on m7's WIP placement but -0.7% (stream) and -1.5% (share) at 1T on main 387034c with the same stream-loop code at the same pinned offset, and is neutral at 48T: not merged (section F). Mechanism: ~-30% cocsep L2 misses (cache model) = ~+1%, paid back in 1-2 µops and ~1 cycle. Calibrations: ~0.15-0.2% per cycle of index-chain latency; ~12 cycles (1% per 140K per cube) per extra cocsep L2 miss. Working-set audit (section G): the table lines insert 78% of the L2 lines and the cocsep entries are their victim; all other structures together insert 5.6%.

What differs is the placement of the other code and of the static tables (HOT_TABLES at 3584/3584 mod 4 KiB on the WIP builds, 896 vs 768 on 387034c: different L1 set mapping). Pinning HOT_TABLES to a 4 KiB boundary (and the cold functions' order) might remove another +-1% confounder from kernel A/Bs.

Idea 7.4 mapped onto our index: the h11 index has no permutation ranks (only base-3 co digits, C(8,4) and C(12,4) combination ranks and eo bits), so popcount Lehmer and Myrvold-Ruskey have nothing to apply to; digit-parallel ranking is what the pext + LUT code already does; the half-split applies to cocsep (ck2). The eslot C(12,4) rank cannot be half-split cheaply: the fixed slot numbering has gaps (slot = r + 2 + 2*floor(r/62)), which would cost an imul + shift instead of one 8 KB LUT load.

G. Hot working-set audit (L1/L2 by structure)

Method: cachegrind with a cache model of one Zen 5 core (--D1=49152,12,64 --LL=1048576,16,64: the "LL" is the 1 MB L2), main b3f0bfb built for x86-64-v3 with scalar stand-ins for the AVX-512 helpers (a diagnostic branch, not retained), bench random:40:2026 40 1 24 (1 thread, 24 lanes). Misses are attributed to source lines by a script that is not published. Cachegrind ignores prefetches, so a prefetched line is counted at its first load; the lines brought into L2 (= L2 misses) are the same. No L3 is modelled.

Ranking and what shrinking each could give:

  1. The h11 table lines are the dominant inserter (one dead line per lookup; inherent). They are what evicts the cocsep entries. Keeping them out of L2 would help most, but the only tool, CLFLUSHOPT after use, costs -30% (neg/xvc-clflushopt-table-lines). NTA is already the hint (T0/T1 measured worse, m6).
  2. The cocsep entries are the victim: 20% of their reads miss the modelled L2 (84% miss L1). Measured: +33% footprint (s4) = -1.6% at 1T; 18-bit entries in rows of 72 (p18d, -21%): +1.2% / -0.7% at 1T depending on placement, share -1.5%, 48T neutral: the ~+1% footprint gain is paid back in µops.
  3. Everything else together inserts 5.6% of the L2 lines. Even removing all of it would cut the cocsep misses by at most ~6% (~30K of 509K per cube), i.e. ~0.1-0.2% at the measured cost of a cocsep miss. A smaller ChildOrder does not help per run (each run touches one 64-byte line whatever the size); it would only halve the per-iteration scans (24K misses per cube). Not worth a patch.
  4. L1: after the table and cocsep, the 8 KB eslot_complement table is the largest L1 miss source (12.8% of its reads; 495 valid entries over 99 of its 128 lines). Shrinking it needs a 2-level rank (two 64-entry half tables + a 495-entry rank->slot table: +5 µops and a dependent load), which costs more than its L2-hit latency (mostly off the critical msplit path). Not pursued.

Calibration (cache model, same instruction count): s4 adds 226K cocsep L2 misses per cube (hit rate 80% -> 71%) and measured -1.6% at 1T: about 1% per 140K misses per cube (~12 cycles per extra miss). p18 removes 183K per cube (hit rate 87%) for +0.46% Ir (v3 build): the model predicts about +1%.

48 threads: L2 is per core, so the per-core picture is the same. The cocsep table is one shared copy in the CCD's L3 (a cocsep L2 miss is an L3 hit at 1T and 48T), but at 48T eight cores churn the L3 with table lines (~8 x 55M lines/s per CCD), so an L3 hit is slower under load and a cocsep L2 miss costs more: the footprint gain of p18 should be at least as large at 48T. The per-lane job state (24 lanes x ~2 MB per core, ~380 MB per CCD) cannot stay in L3 at 48T; each run start then reads its job and ChildOrder lines from DRAM (prefetched one run ahead in setup_run), ~50K runs per cube: small next to 2.4M table misses.
