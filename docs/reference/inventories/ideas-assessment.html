Assessment of optimization ideas from Tom Rokicki and Arhan Chaudhary (2026-11-04)

Historical snapshot. Its source, timings and pending statements belong to the recorded experiment, not the current checkout. Documentation home.

This table assesses a numbered list of optimization ideas from Tom Rokicki and Arhan Chaudhary. The list itself is not published; the first column keeps its numbering, which the other inventories cite as “idea 2.4” and similar. The Action column records the follow-up planned at the time, with the recorded outcome where one of the other inventories measured it.

The solver at the time: H48 h11, 2-bit entries, base 10, 4-bit group minima; IDA* with 24 lanes per thread.

Assessment of optimization ideas from Tom Rokicki and Arhan Chaudhary (2026-11-04)
#IdeaStatus in our solverAction
1.1–1.5Faster table generation (scanning, batched writes, symmetry at the root)Affects only generation time. Our limit is RAM, not generation time.Use in the new any-k gen for laptop-sized tables.
2.1nxopt 2-bit + per-line min + baseDone. cube48opt-style 16-byte groups with a 4-bit min; base 10 confirmed best for h11 (simulations in the nxopt and nissy-core inventories: base 11 +38%, base 9 +268% nodes).none
2.2–2.4, 2.6Denser or entropy-coded tables (trits 1.25x, bitmaps, tANS ~1.5x, lossy)They only pay if the freed memory buys a bigger effective table, and H48 tops out at h11 (full EO).Feasibility study of a "bigger effective table within ~65 GiB": a coset refinement × an encoding, with the expected nodes gain and decode cost. Outcome: infeasible (nissy-core inventory, feasibility section).
2.7Non-power-of-two sizes without divisionN/A: exact index, no hashing.none
3.1–3.4One big table; mean h is the key metric; nodes × size ≈ constantKnown; it guides the study above.—
3.6Small exact in-cache table that catches many lookups (untried)We have only the cocsep corner-depth precheck.Measure the fraction of h11 lookups a cache-resident projection table (≤ L2/L3 size) would prune. Outcome: 0.16% of lookups; negative (nxopt inventory, M6).
3.8Table checksum before/after runsWe have table-meta validation, tabcheck and a read-only mapping.Optional --verify-table (xxhash) for the final comparison.
4.1, 4.5, 4.6, 4.7Inverse probe; rotated probes; fast representative; mirroringDone, or N/A for a 48-symmetric table.—
4.2–4.4Merge the inverse into the table (96-way)It needs a coset structure closed under inversion. The H48 coset of p and of p⁻¹ differ, and the mod-3 encoding is invalid on the inverse side.Part of the bigger-table feasibility study above.
4.8Symmetric inputs: search the first levels slowly, drop symmetric duplicates (symmetry² speedup)Symmetric start was being implemented (later merged as f07b109).Use Rokicki's deeper version; we report one solution, so the all-solutions caveat doesn't apply.
4.9Symmetry duplicates during searchNot worth it (confirmed).—
5.2BPMX / pathmax (full: return early from the parent)We have sibling-face skip and back-jumps (lanes.rs doc).Verify that the full BPMX early return happens on both sides, including against premoves (the inverse side); if not, measure it. Outcome: in-tree BPMX is complete; job-level pathmax would skip 0 jobs (nxopt inventory, M4).
5.3Continue in the direction with fewer allowed movesDone (side choice by popcount, inverse-first).—
5.4Canonical sequences beyond commuting (twsearch --newcanon, longer identities)Commuting order only. Depth-4 duplicates 0.035%, depth-5 0.43% (vcube inventory); an earlier estimate said ≤ 0.5%.Measure duplicate sequences in the real search tree at depths 5–10 (positions reached by >1 canonical sequence within a job) and read twsearch's newcanon source. Implement a DFA if it pays ≥ 1%. Outcome: at most 0.48% of nodes, below the 1% threshold (nxopt inventory, M5).
5.5, 6.1, 6.2, 6.3Preallocated stacks; deep splits; microthreading; huge pagesDone: lanes = microthreading; 1 GiB pages; 2–4-move job prefixes.—
7.2min(v, v−3) mod-3 fix; SIMD inverseDone (vcube inventory: our move 4.6 Ir, child cubes 12.5 Ir, vs vcube's 4.4 and 53.8).—
7.4Ranking: popcount Lehmer, half-split tables, digit parallelismOur index chain is 45 Ir and 15.2 ns latency (vcube inventory).Apply these to shorten the index chain. Outcome: the index has no permutation ranks for popcount Lehmer ranking, digit-parallel ranking was already in use, and the half-split variant was negative (vcube inventory, section F).
7.1, 7.5Throughput over many positions; Apple Silicon differences—Laptop/NEON port.
